# Security baseline

Who the application defends against and what it accepts (the threat model and the exception register, step M8.3), then what it enforces, area by area, step by step (M1.3 onwards) and where. Decisions behind it: [ADR-0004](adr/0004-session-auth-allauth-mfa.md), [ADR-0009](adr/0009-no-redis-database-cache-outbox.md), [ADR-0011](adr/0011-authorize-in-transaction-principals.md). Review passes, their findings and the ASVS checklist: [security-review.md](security-review.md).

## Threat model

### What is protected

- **Business data of each company, branch and project**: requests, orders, receipts, handovers, and their quantities.
- **Commercial terms**: prices and discounts, which only `price.view` reaches. Supplier records and contacts are behind `supplier.view`.
- **Personal data**: names, addresses and phone numbers of staff and partner users, client addresses in the audit trail and the logs, and photos and delivery notes in attachments.
- **The integrity of the quantity ledger**: what was approved, ordered, received and delivered, and who did it. This is where fraud would show.
- **Credentials and keys**:
  - password hashes; MFA secrets (encrypted with `MFA_ENCRYPTION_KEYS`); session keys;
  - the invitation, email-change and reset tokens;
  - `SECRET_KEY` and `OUTBOX_ENCRYPTION_KEYS`;
  - the three database role passwords and the SMTP password;
  - the restic password and the offsite credentials;
  - the GitHub account the agents use, and the release signing identity (keyless, the publish workflow's).

### Actors and boundaries

**Anonymous visitors on the internet.** They reach only the sign-in and reset pages, the token links, the health checks and static files. Caddy terminates TLS (1.2 or later), limits headers and bodies, and is the only published port. Against them:
- every page carries the CSP and the other headers;
- sign-in, MFA codes, resets and token links are rate-limited on a cache that fails closed;
- no response tells whether an account exists;
- tokens are 256-bit, hashed at rest and single use.

**Roles inside the group.** Every user is one of the seven roles of plan §3.4, each limited to a scope: a branch and the branches it manages, assigned projects, or their own requests.
- Scope is checked three times: by the view (403 without the capability, 404 outside the scope), by the selectors (`visible_to`) and inside every write (`authorize_in_txn`, with the transaction's clock and locks). A revocation therefore applies to the next write.
- Capabilities never mix across memberships.
- The acceptance matrix calls every URL and method as every actor, and checks each response for the sentinels it must not show.
- Residual risk: a page that is already loading when a revocation lands still renders (plan §3.6).

**The partner boundary.** `PARTNER_REQUESTER` users belong to another company (C). They see their assigned, active projects and their own requests, and never a price, a supplier, an order or another person's request.
- The matrix checks their pages for every sentinel.
- Their email (notices of their own requests) is filtered the same way.
- Deactivating their company ends their access (S9).
- Residual: they see the group's catalog (`catalog.view` is every role's, so that they can request materials) and the names of their projects' reviewers.

**Insiders.**
- **Technicians and site managers.** A technician sees only their own requests. A site manager sees the expected-deliveries projection of their assigned projects. That projection carries no prices, no supplier contacts and no other project's quantities; M8.3 closed a path that leaked the latter (security-review.md, R2).
- **Material managers and engineers.** They buy and receive in their branches, and may decide their own requests (ADR-0014). Such a decision is marked `self_decided: true` in the append-only audit, and returning or reducing their own request counts as one (M8.3). Their sessions need MFA, last 12 hours, and end after 30 minutes idle.
- **Group administrators.** They see and manage everything in the installation: people, roles, invitations, email changes. They cannot act on staff or superuser accounts, cannot take over an account without notice (an email change waits 24 hours, and the old address can cancel), and cannot read a password hash, a token or an MFA secret (the admin hides them and refuses lookups on them, M8.3). Everything they change is audited, and the runtime database role cannot alter the audit.
- **Platform operators (superusers).** They are break-glass accounts. `is_superuser` grants no business capability. The admin shows them master data and documents read-only, without prices. In practice they also hold the server, which is covered below.
- **Residual.** A group administrator, or a privileged role within its branches, can still abuse what the business gives it: approve and buy for its own requests, or read every document in its scope. Detection rests on the audit trail and the security log (`gmat.security`, M8.3), which a group administrator can read but not alter.

**The agent boundary.** AI coding agents write code, push branches and open PRs.
- In the interim setup (Q20) they use the maintainer's GitHub account. That account is an administrator and the bypass actor of the tag ruleset, so GitHub would not stop it from merging, tagging or releasing.
- What stops an agent: the rules of `CLAUDE.md`, the deny list of `.claude/settings.json` (tightened in M8.3 and kept by a policy test), the `main` ruleset (no direct push, force push or deletion), the workflow policy tests, and the human review of every PR, `.github/workflows` changes in particular.
- Publishing needs a maintainer-pushed `v*` tag, a commit on `main` and green CI, compose-smoke and e2e runs on that exact commit; `deploy.sh` verifies both signatures and the manifest before it touches anything.
- A prompt injection in content an agent reads is the realistic path to misuse. The guards above apply to it as well.
- Accepted until the dedicated agent account and fork exist: exception X2.

**A compromised VPS.** Root on the server holds the live database, the media volume, the local backups and every runtime secret (`SECRET_KEY`, the MFA and outbox keys, the database and SMTP passwords, the restic password and the append-only offsite credentials). Such an attacker can read everything, sign sessions, and send mail as the application.
- What they cannot do:
  - delete or rewrite the offsite backups: the VPS holds append-only credentials, pruning runs only from the maintainer's machine with `--keep-within 90d`, and a pre-prune check refuses forged snapshots (`scripts/offsite_prune.py`), judged also against what the maintainer's machine saw at the last prune, which the VPS cannot reach;
  - publish an image or a release: the VPS holds a read-only token, and images and manifests are signed by the publish workflow;
  - change the backups taken before the compromise: they sit offsite, behind append-only credentials.
- Recovery: rebuild from the release images and the offsite backup, rotate every secret, and run `recover.sh restore-offsite`, which ends in `post-restore-reconcile` (runbooks in [runbooks/](runbooks/README.md)).
- Containers limit what a compromised process reaches: uid 1000, read-only root, no capabilities, one database network per client, and no route out for `web`. What parses uploaded files, `clamd` and the image decoder, has no network at all and none of these secrets (ADR-0015): an exploit there starts with nothing.

**The supply chain.**
- Dependencies come from `uv.lock` with hashes, with security floors (allauth), `pip-audit` and Dependabot.
- Base images are pinned by digest and scanned by Trivy on every PR and every night, Caddy, PostgreSQL and ClamAV included, with dated exceptions (X1).
- Actions are pinned by commit SHA, and gitleaks scans the whole history.

**Out of scope.** The security of users' devices and mailboxes; the email provider (which sees every mail; supplier dispatches carry prices by design); DNS and the domain's mail authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC: H8.6). Unrelated customer groups get separate deployments (ADR-0003).

## Authentication (`config/settings/base.py`, `apps/accounts/`)

- django-allauth (floor 65.20.1, checked in CI and by system check `gmat.E001`) is the only authentication backend. Login is by email and password, then MFA when the account has a factor.
- **Closed surface.** Sign-up, allauth's email management and email confirmation, login by code and "set first password" have no URL (`apps/accounts/urls.py`). Accounts arrive through invitations and email addresses change through the administrator flow (both below); `createsuperuser` is disabled and the first administrator comes from `manage bootstrap_admin`.
- **No enumeration.** A wrong password and an unknown email give the same answer; a reset request for an unknown or inactive address sends nothing and looks the same.
- **Logout is POST-only** and answers with `Clear-Site-Data: "cache", "storage"`.
- **Password reset**: one-hour token, never logs anyone in, and is redeemed as a password-reset `TokenPrincipal` (`AccountAdapter.redeem_password_reset`): `authorize_in_txn` locks the user row and the token is checked again under that lock, so two concurrent redemptions cannot both succeed. Inactive accounts are refused.
- **Clean slate per login.** A new anonymous login discards any half-finished login in the session, and the login signal keeps only the authentication records of the account that logged in.
- Passwords: Argon2id, minimum length 12.
- **Credential changes tell the owner** (M8.3). A password changed or reset, a second factor added or removed and recovery codes generated each send allauth's notice, and an administrator's MFA reset sends one too: through the outbox, sealed like any account mail, with the address and browser it came from, only while the account is active and still has that address (`gmat.E002` keeps it on).

## Sessions (`apps/accounts/sessions.py`, `middleware.py`)

- Each session stores the user's `session_epoch`, its login time and its last activity. `SessionGuardMiddleware` ends a session whose epoch is stale, whose lifetime is over (12 hours for privileged accounts: platform operators and holders of a valid `GROUP_ADMIN`, `MATERIAL_MANAGER` or `ENGINEER` membership, `apps.access.policy.is_privileged`; 14 days for the others) or that stayed idle too long (30 minutes for privileged accounts, 2 hours for the others; M8.3). All four come from the environment. The security log says why (`session_ended`, `reason`).
- **What counts as activity.** Every request, except what a page sends by itself (`sessions.session_passive`, the delivery badges' polls), so a page left open cannot keep a session alive. It is written at most once a minute.
- **A person's own sessions** (`/account/sessions/`, `allauth.usersessions` for the rows, our own view and services). Anyone signed in, whatever their memberships (`OWN_ACCOUNT`), with session MFA on a privileged account, lists where they are signed in and ends one session or all the others; someone else's id answers like a session already ended. The list shows what could really still be used: each row is checked against its session with the guard's own rules. Rows hold an address and a browser, so they go with a logout, at the hourly `sessions` housekeeping, which also deletes expired sessions, and in every restore. Dumps leave them out, and the admin does not show them. An ended session is audited (`user.sessions_ended`) and needs no replay after a restore, which ends every session anyway.
- A database trigger (`accounts.0002`) bumps `session_epoch` on a password change, an email change and a deactivation, and keeps `session_epoch` and `authz_generation` from ever decreasing. `authz_generation` is bumped when `is_active`, `is_staff`, `is_superuser` or `employer_company` changes, and (`access.0002`) when a membership or project assignment of the user is created or changed.
- A password change and an MFA reset (`apps.accounts.mfa.reset_mfa`) end every session of the user; removing one's own factor ends the user's other sessions.
- Cookies: `__Host-sessionid` and `__Host-csrftoken`, `Secure`, `SameSite=Lax`, both `HttpOnly` (htmx reads the CSRF token from the page). `dev` uses plain names over HTTP.

## MFA

- TOTP and recovery codes; passkeys (WebAuthn) as an optional second factor, never as a password replacement. "Trust this browser" is off and TOTP accepts only the current 30-second step, which allauth then marks as used.
- **Session-level check** (`apps.accounts.mfa.session_passed_mfa`): the *current session* must have completed MFA after the user's last privilege elevation, with an authenticator that belongs to the logged-in user. An enrolled but unused factor does not count.
- **Role-based enforcement** (`apps.accounts.mfa.mfa_required_for`, `MFARequiredMiddleware`): for a privileged user every URL needs that check, except allauth's account and MFA pages (to complete MFA or sign out), the language switch and the health checks. A GET is sent to MFA reauthentication (or enrollment, without a factor); anything else gets 403. Inside every write, `authorize_in_txn` checks it again whenever only a privileged grant covers the operation.
- **Step-up.** Granting a privileged role sets `privilege_elevated_at` to the grant's database time (a role that starts later included), so every existing session of that user steps up before using any privileged grant.
- **Secrets at rest**: `MFAAdapter` encrypts TOTP secrets and recovery-code seeds with `MultiFernet` over `MFA_ENCRYPTION_KEYS`. allauth's default adapter stores them in plaintext (a test proves it).
- **Key rotation**: put a new key first in `MFA_ENCRYPTION_KEYS` and keep the old ones; new secrets use the new key and old ones still decrypt. No command re-encrypts existing secrets yet, so an old key stays listed until every account has been reset or has re-enrolled; rotation after a leak means resetting everyone's factors ([runbook](runbooks/user-admin.md#encryption-keys-mfa-and-outbox)). Keep an offline copy of the keys; losing all of them means every user re-enrolls TOTP.
- Privileged accounts cannot remove their last second factor, whether they deactivate their TOTP or remove a passkey. Since M8.3 the passkey path asks too: `views.remove_webauthn` replaces allauth's view, which never did.
- **Lost factors** (M8.3). `manage reset_mfa --operator <admin> --email <account> --reason "…"` (`services.reset_user_mfa`) removes every factor of the account and ends all its sessions, audited as `user.mfa_reset` with the reason. It needs `org.manage` (a superuser for a staff account), and nobody resets their own factors. The account's next sign-in enrolls a new factor where its roles need one. Check the person's identity first (runbook M8.5).

## Admin (`apps/accounts/admin_site.py`, `apps/core/admin.py`)

- Logging in to the admin goes through allauth (`secure_admin_login` sends anonymous visitors to its login page); Django's admin login form is never processed. Admin URLs need an active superuser or a holder of `org.manage` (a group administrator) as the access engine resolves it (`apps.access.authorization.may_use_admin`), and a session that completed MFA after the last elevation: a GET is sent to MFA reauthentication (or enrollment), anything else gets 403.
- Whoever the site admits may view every registered model. Only master data (companies, joint-venture members, branches, locations) can be added or changed, and only by a group administrator (`org.manage`, through `apps.access.admin.CapabilityModelAdmin`, per object for a scoped model); a platform operator without that membership only views. `is_superuser` grants no business capability. Users, memberships, projects, assignments and the access state are read-only, so a group administrator cannot edit a staff account or its flags; the services also refuse to change a staff account's memberships, assignments or employer unless the actor is a superuser.
- Every `ModelAdmin` is built on `ServiceBackedModelAdmin` (system check `gmat.E005`): saves go through a service, deletes and the bulk delete action do not exist. Users, email addresses and authenticators are read-only, and MFA secrets and password hashes are never displayed. Django's groups are not registered.
- The admin's password-change pages redirect to allauth's, so a password changes in one place, under its rate limit (M2.4). Admin logout hands over to allauth's logout the same way.
- **Lists filter only on what they offer** (M8.3). Django accepts a query-string lookup on any local field, so a list's result count could read back, one guess at a time, a column its pages hide: a password hash, a token hash, an MFA secret, a price. `ServiceBackedModelAdmin.lookup_allowed` accepts only the fields of the admin's list filters and date hierarchy; anything else is a 400. The audit admin shows neither an event's payload (which may hold price changes) nor the client address, like the in-app viewer.

## Authorization (`apps/access/`, step M2.3)

- **Capability matrix as data** (`roles.py`, plan §3.4): `ROLE_CAPABILITIES` maps each role to its capabilities with a reach: `SCOPE` (●, everything in the membership's scope), `ASSIGNED` (○, the projects assigned to a site manager or partner) or `OWN` (○ own, the user's own documents). A test compares it with the plan's table. Catalog and supplier capabilities reach the installation-wide master data only.
- **Scopes** (`context.py`, `scopes.py`, plan §3.5): `AccessContext.scope_for(capability)` returns one grant per valid membership and never merges them (S6). S1: a branch and the active branches it manages. S2: everything for group administrators, inactive history included. S3: projects of a branch scope, by branch or procurement branch. S4: assigned projects (partners and technicians see active projects only). S5: documents by branch or project, own documents only for `OWN`. S8: half-open validity at the database clock, for an active, not offboarded user. S9: inactive companies and branches grant nothing and drop out of every scope; a partner membership grants nothing while its company is inactive, nor do the assignments that depend on it. A hypothesis test checks the resolution against a reference implementation on random organization graphs.
- **Reads**: `request.access` (`AccessContextMiddleware`) is built per request and reads nothing until asked; `Model.objects.visible_to(ctx, capability)` exists for `Branch`, `Project` and `Location`, and document models add theirs.
- **Views**: `require(request, capability, obj, read=..., methods=...)` answers 405 for another method, 404 for an object the user cannot see and 403 for one they see but may not act on (S7). `@require_capability` and `CapabilityRequiredMixin` add the login and coarse check; `@mutating` and `MutatingViewMixin` also accept POST only. `{% can %}` only hides UI.
- **Writes** (`authorization.py`, plan §3.6, S10): every mutating service calls `authorize_in_txn(principal, capability, *objects)` inside its transaction, in full every time. It locks `AccessState` and the actor's `User` row `FOR SHARE`, requires an active user whose session epoch is current (`StaleSession` otherwise, and the session is flushed), reloads the memberships at the transaction's clock, requires every object in the capability's scope (with `read`, an invisible object is a 404), and requires session MFA after the last elevation when only privileged grants cover the operation. Revocations take the same rows `FOR UPDATE` or `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, so they wait for the writes in flight and apply to every later write.
- **Principals** (`principals.py`): `SessionPrincipal` (the session's epoch and MFA time), `TokenPrincipal` (one operation; the target and initiator rows are locked in id order before the token's own check, and the initiator must still hold `org.manage`, a superuser for a staff target), `SystemPrincipal` (`outbox.send`, `housekeeping.*`, no business capability) and `OperatorPrincipal` (the command's one capability, `via=cli:<command>`, no session MFA). The last three raise `PrincipalNotAllowed` when constructed outside `CONSTRUCTOR_MODULES` (`apps.accounts.tokens`, `apps.accounts.adapters`, `apps.notifications.worker`, `apps.core.commands`).
- **Other rules** (`policy.py`): `is_superuser` is read only in `apps/access` and grants no business capability; only a superuser acts on a staff account. Self-decisions are checked by `authorization.authorize_self_decision` (see M7b.1 below).

## Guardrails and the acceptance matrix (step M2.4)

- **Static guard** (`scripts/check_access_rules.py`, CI `lint` job, stdlib only). Text rules: `|safe`, `mark_safe`, `csrf_exempt`, `.raw(`, `.extra(`, `is_superuser` in templates, and the `pull_request_target` / `workflow_run` triggers. Parsed rules over `apps/` and `config/` (comments and docstrings never match):
  - `scoped-model-access`: a scoped model's manager (`objects`, `_default_manager`) or `get_object_or_404(<ScopedModel>, …)` in views, forms or template tags. A scoped model is any model whose manager is built on `ScopedQuerySet`, found by reading the models, so document models are covered as soon as they exist;
  - `is_superuser`: an attribute, lookup, keyword or `getattr` outside `apps/access`;
  - `service-without-authorize`: a public function in a `services` module that writes (`@transactional`, an ORM write, `apply_changes`) without reaching `authorize_in_txn` directly, through its module's helpers or through another service module's function. Helpers that write for an authorized caller are private (`_elevate_privilege`);
  - `get-view-calls-service`: a view function or class that reaches a service without being POST-only (`@mutating`, `@require_POST`, `post_only=True`, `require_http_methods(["POST"])`, `MutatingViewMixin`, `http_method_names = ["post"]`);
  - `plain-model-admin`: a class based on `ModelAdmin`, or `site.register(Model)` without an admin class (the base itself is allowlisted);
  - `principal-constructor`: `TokenPrincipal`, `SystemPrincipal` or `OperatorPrincipal` constructed outside the modules listed in `CONSTRUCTOR_MODULES`, which the guard reads from `apps/access/principals.py` (an unreadable registry fails the check);
  - `mail-outside-outbox`: `smtplib`, or Django's `send_mail`, `send_mass_mail`, `mail_admins`, `mail_managers`, `EmailMessage`, `EmailMultiAlternatives` or `get_connection` outside `apps.notifications.worker`.

  The runtime checks stay: `gmat.E005` for admin classes, `PrincipalNotAllowed` in the principals' constructors.
- **Methods.** Every URL answers 405 to anything but GET, HEAD and POST (`AllowedMethodsMiddleware`, inside `ErrorPagesMiddleware` so the 405 has its page); views narrow further.
- **Shared fixture** (`tests/org.py`, plan §8.1): the A/B/C organization of §3.2, one user per role, a user who is material manager in A-SEV and technician in A-MAD, expired, revoked, revoked-assignment, inactive-branch, inactive-company-partner and deactivated users, and sentinel values (a price, a supplier's name, email and phone, another project's quantity). Numbers are matched in localized renderings (`98.765,43`, `98,765.43`, grouped with no-break spaces), names case-insensitively, phones with or without the country code. Suppliers (M3.2) and orders (M5.1) seed them.
- **Acceptance matrix** (`tests/access/test_acceptance_matrix.py`, plan §8.2): one row per URL pattern (keyed by URL name, or by route when unnamed) with the expected status and redirect target for GET, HEAD and POST, for each of 20 actors: anonymous, every role, the mixed-membership user, privileged sessions with and without MFA, the platform operator, the revoked cases and a session whose password changed. Every response is checked for the sentinels its row does not allow. A URL without a row, or a row without a URL, fails the test; other methods are checked to be 405 everywhere. URLs with an object list one row per object position (own scope, managed branch, foreign scope). The admin is checked too: every admin service-backed, access and document models read-only, no bulk delete, a master-data save through its service (generation bumped, audit written), a group administrator refused on a staff account, and an admin write refused after a membership expiry or revocation, a password, email or MFA change, a deactivation or an elevation without step-up between page load and submit.
- **The matrix checks itself** (`tests/access/test_matrix_guards.py`): views that commit each violation of M2.4's "done when" (an unscoped view, an unregistered URL, a leaked price, a GET view calling a service, a system principal in a view, a plain `ModelAdmin`) make it fail.
- Found by the matrix: the passkey list crashed for every signed-in user (`humanize` missing), and the admin's own password-change form bypassed allauth. Both are fixed.

## Rate limits and the security cache (`apps/core/cache.py`)

- allauth's rate limits (`ACCOUNT_RATE_LIMITS`) use the default cache, `SecurityDatabaseCache` over the migrated `django_cache` table: atomic `add` and `incr` under row locks, no eviction of live entries, JSON values (no pickle), bounded lock waits, and a capacity of `SECURITY_CACHE_MAX_ENTRIES`. At capacity, or when a lock wait times out, the write fails and the request gets **429**: authentication fails closed. The lock companion of a live counter is always admitted, so existing counters keep working.
- The client IP comes from `apps.core.http.client_ip`: with `ALLAUTH_TRUSTED_PROXY_COUNT = 1` only the address Caddy appends to `X-Forwarded-For` counts; forged entries further left are ignored.

## Logging (`config/logging.py`)

- One JSON handler on stdout. Every handler carries `RedactionFilter` (system check `gmat.E004`): token paths (the password-reset key, the segment after `/invite/` and `/account/email-change/`, and any long random path segment) become `REDACTED`, query strings and fragments are dropped, `secret=value` pairs are masked, and the `request` attribute keeps only the method and the redacted path. `Referer` and `HX-Current-URL` are never logged.
- No `AdminEmailHandler` reaches Django's loggers. Error reports hide authentication POST bodies and URL-carrying headers (`RedactingExceptionReporterFilter`). In production no error report is rendered at all (DEBUG is refused, and no handler mails one). The technical error page of a development server still shows the full URL. `scrub_event` is the `before_send` hook for an optional error tracker (use it with `send_default_pii=False`).
- **Who and what** (M8.3, ASVS V16.3).
  - Each line written while a view runs names the signed-in account (`user`, its id; `apps.access.middleware`), so a refused request (403, 404) shows who made it.
  - `apps.accounts.security_log` writes one `gmat.security` line per security event: sign-in (with how: `password`, `mfa:totp`…), failed sign-in, sign-out, password change and reset, second factor added, removed or regenerated, refused MFA code. Each line carries the account id and the client address Caddy saw.
  - A failed sign-in never carries the address typed, only a keyed hash of it (`salted_hmac` with `SECRET_KEY`), so attempts on one account can be grouped.
- The sentinel test plants a secret in a reset URL, an invitation URL, an email-change URL, a password field and the `Referer` and `HX-Current-URL` headers, on 404, 500 and normal responses, and checks every log line.

## Email: the outbox and the worker (step M2.5)

Code in `apps/notifications/`; plan §5 "Email: outbox and worker".

- **No SMTP in a request.** `MAILERS["default"]` is `OutboxEmailBackend` in every environment (checks `gmat.E007`, and `gmat.E017` in production): Django's and allauth's mail becomes `EmailOutbox` rows in the caller's transaction. Business code queues through `apps.notifications.outbox` (`queue_notification`, `queue_security_email`, `queue_dispatch`). The static guard keeps `smtplib` and Django's sending API inside `apps.notifications.worker`.
- **Frozen content, re-checked recipients.** Subject, bodies, recipients and eligibility are frozen at insert by a trigger (`notifications.0002`; a body may only be wiped). Notifications store user ids and a capability on an object, at most 20 per message (BCC); the worker re-checks each recipient's own access at the database clock of the claim and resolves addresses from the current user records, dropping who no longer qualifies (`SUPPRESSED` when nobody is left). allauth's mail is a `SECURITY` email to one account, sent only while that account is active and still has the address.
- **Sensitive bodies** (security emails) are sealed with `OUTBOX_ENCRYPTION_KEYS` (MultiFernet, their own keys; never an MFA key, `gmat.E018`), wiped as soon as the message is sent or can no longer be sent, and wiped and `EXPIRED` one hour after queueing if still unsent (hourly housekeeping).
- **The worker** (`manage.py worker`, `entrypoint.sh worker`) is the only sender and runs as `system:worker` (`outbox.send`, `housekeeping.*`, no business capability). Each message: claim in a short transaction (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`; a supplier dispatch locks its order first, L4 → L4b), eligibility re-check, an `EmailAttempt` committed **before** any network I/O; send with a 10 s timeout per socket operation (`OUTBOX_SMTP_TIMEOUT`; Django reserves `EMAIL_TIMEOUT`) and a 60 s total deadline enforced by `SIGALRM`; the outcome is recorded only while `current_attempt` is still that attempt. `FAILED` = refused connection or definite rejection before DATA; `AMBIGUOUS` = timeout or disconnect after DATA started. An attempt without outcome 90 s after it started (worker crash) becomes `AMBIGUOUS`, checked at start-up, every minute and exactly when the oldest attempt in flight becomes due. Retries: notifications `FAILED` up to 5 attempts with backoff and `AMBIGUOUS` once; security emails `FAILED` within their hour, never after `AMBIGUOUS`; supplier dispatches never after `AMBIGUOUS`. Errors record a stage and an SMTP code, never the server's text; logs carry message ids only.
- **Switches.** `flags/worker.paused`: the worker only writes its heartbeat (no query, claim, send or housekeeping). `OUTBOUND_EMAIL=off` (default in production until set): it never claims or opens an SMTP connection. Restore drills set it off.
- **Housekeeping** (hourly, one transaction and one `housekeeping.<task>` authorization per task): idempotency records past retention, expired security-cache rows, expired sensitive bodies; since M6.4 also expired attachment reservations and the media purge (below).
- **Containers.** The worker shares no network with `web` (`db_worker` and `egress` only); the SMTP password reaches the worker container only. Development, local and CI stacks deliver to Mailpit, so no mail leaves the host.

## Accounts: invitations, email changes, offboarding (step M2.6)

Code in `apps/accounts/` (`tokens`, `services`, `revocation`, `housekeeping`, `eligibility`, the token views) and the screens in `apps/organizations/views.py`; plan §3.6 (principals) and §5.

- **Tokens.** 32 random bytes (`secrets.token_urlsafe`), shown only in an email or, for `bootstrap_admin`, on the operator's terminal. The database keeps the SHA-256; lookups compare it again with `hmac.compare_digest`. Invitations live 72 hours, email confirmations 24 hours. A link stores the hash in the session and redirects to a token-less page, so the token leaves the address bar, the history and any `Referer` on the first hit; the link views are exempt from the MFA step-up so the token never lands in a `next` parameter. Links and submissions share a rate limit (20 a minute per client IP). A link over it is never answered where the token sits: it stores nothing and redirects to the token-less page, which answers the 429 (M8.3). Every expired, used, revoked or unknown token shows one generic page and forgets the session's token.
- **Redemption** runs as a persistent-token `TokenPrincipal`: `authorize_in_txn` locks `AccessState`, then the target and initiator `User` rows in id order, then the token row, and checks the token's state, the target rules and that the **initiator still holds `org.manage`** (a superuser for a staff target). Offboarding locks the same `User` rows first, so either order ends with the account inactive and no valid membership (race tests in `tests/integration/test_invitation_races.py`, with two concurrent redemptions of one token).
- **Invitation kinds.** A new account is created signed out with a password (validators, minimum 12); the unique email stops a parallel creation. An active account accepts only while signed in as itself, and its credentials never change. An inactive account is re-hired only by an invitation created after its `offboarded_at` (the invitation's time is the database wall clock after its locks); the old authenticators are removed. Every granted branch, company and project must still be active. Privileged roles move `privilege_elevated_at`, so MFA is enrolled before they are used. A group administrator cannot invite a staff or superuser account.
- **Email changes** are started by an administrator (`org.manage`; a superuser for staff). The new address confirms (single use, 24 h) and the old one receives a cancel link valid until the change applies. Confirmation starts a 24-hour cooling-off; the worker's hourly housekeeping (`email_changes`, as `system:worker`) applies it under the same locks only if the account is active, the initiator still holds the authority and the address is still free, otherwise revokes it. Applying replaces the address and its verified `EmailAddress`, and the user trigger ends every session.
- **Offboarding** (one transaction): `is_active = false`, `offboarded_at`, the triggers bump `authz_generation` and `session_epoch`; every membership and assignment revoked; every pending invitation and email change for the account (by user and by address) and every one it issued revoked; unsent security emails expired and wiped. Revoking or moving away a `GROUP_ADMIN` membership that leaves the user without `org.manage` revokes their issued tokens too. Administrators cannot offboard themselves.
- **Mail.** Invitation and email-change messages are sensitive `SECURITY` emails with their own rules (`invitation.pending`, `email_change.confirmable`, `email_change.cancellable`), checked again at the worker's claim. Inactive accounts receive no password-reset email.
- **First administrator.** `manage bootstrap_admin` runs only while no active `GROUP_ADMIN` exists (and, with `--superuser`, no active superuser); it creates the company and head office if missing and a `NEW_ACCOUNT` invitation granting `GROUP_ADMIN`, audited `via=bootstrap`. Running it again replaces an unused link; acceptance checks again that no administrator appeared meanwhile.

## Master data: catalog, suppliers, imports (steps M3.1–M3.4)

Code in `apps/catalog/`, `apps/suppliers/`, `apps/core/{tabular,imports,demo}.py` and the `import_*` / `seed_demo` commands; plan §3.4, §4.2, §4.6 (C9).

- **Scope.** Catalog and supplier rows are installation-wide: their models use `InstallationQuerySet`, so a grant reaches all of it or none. The capability decides: `catalog.view` every role with a valid membership, `catalog.manage` group administrators and material managers; `supplier.view` group administrators, material managers and warehouse operators, `supplier.manage` the first two. Site managers, technicians and partners get 403 on every supplier URL and never see a supplier name, email or phone (the supplier sentinels sit in the shared fixture and the matrix checks every response of every actor for them); a site manager meets a supplier's display name only through the expected-deliveries projection of an assigned project (M5/M6). A user with no catalog access at all gets 404 for an object (S7).
- **Writes** go through `apps/catalog/services.py` and `apps/suppliers/services.py`: one transaction, `authorize_in_txn` in full, the idempotency key for creates, validation, `audit.record`, `events.publish`. Uniqueness (reference, names, tax id, category names) is settled by the database; `constraint_errors` turns a violation, including a concurrent insert, into a field error. A material's unit cannot change once a document references it: the composite foreign key refuses the update and the service reports it on the `unit` field (a test builds the referencing table). Nothing is deleted; materials and suppliers are deactivated, and a change that alters nothing writes nothing.
- **Search** folds case and accents in the database (`catalog_fold`, an immutable wrapper of `lower(unaccent())` so it can be indexed), matches every term of the query, escapes `%` and `_` (they are literal), accepts `2,5` and `2.5` alike, and returns at most 20 active materials with no price. Trigram indexes are checked by `EXPLAIN` in the integration suite, with timings on 20,000 rows.
- **Imports** (`manage import_catalog | import_suppliers | import_projects`) are `OperatorCommand`s: `--operator` names an active user who holds the matching capability, no session or MFA exists and the audit shows `via=cli:<command>`. Rows reach the database only through the services, each authorizing the operator again for its own objects, so a material manager cannot import projects outside their branches. The file is read as text (CSV; XLSX in read-only mode with formulas as saved values and `defusedxml` guarding the XML), limited to 20 MB and 100,000 rows. A file with any error writes nothing; an error found while writing rolls the file back, and the report lists errors by line. The report never prints cell values beyond the offending one.
- **`seed_demo`** refuses to run unless `DJANGO_SETTINGS_MODULE` names `config.settings.dev` or `config.settings.test`, before the database is touched. `manage.py` gives an unset variable the development settings, and the guard reads the settings' name, not the database the `DB_*` variables point at. The production image pins `config.settings.prod`, so the command refuses there, but a shell on the server could still run development settings against any database. Demo users get `--password` (validated) or a random one printed once; they are ordinary accounts, so privileged roles still enroll MFA at sign-in.

Code in `apps/material_requests/services/` and `ledger.py`; plan §3.4, §4.3–4.5, §4.9.

## Purchasing: supplier dispatch (step M5.3)

Code in `apps/purchasing/dispatch.py`, `services/dispatches.py` and `templates/purchasing/email/`; plan §4.7 (L4b), §5, Q7.

- **Off by default.** Nothing is sent to a supplier unless `PO_DISPATCH=on` (ADR-0010: the MVP prints the order). The switch gates new `PO_ORDER` dispatches only: the cancellation notice of an order the supplier may already have is always owed.
- **Frozen content.** Recipients and content come from the order's snapshots at issue or amendment (the address the order was issued to, never the live supplier record) and are frozen by the outbox trigger, `Message-ID` included. A line closed short shows what it received, marked closed. The text body is plain text; the HTML body is autoescaped. Prices go to the supplier as recorded on its own order.

Threaded tests show that two workers send one dispatch once and that claim and cancel wait for each other in either order without a deadlock.
- **Eligibility at the claim.** A `PO_ORDER` goes only while it is its order's current revision and the order is not cancelled (else `SUPERSEDED` or `CANCELLED`; an amendment that closes the order still goes out); a `PO_CANCELLATION` only for a cancelled order; one notice per order (unique index). The notice is owed for every dispatch the supplier may have: sent, ambiguous, or queued again by a human after an ambiguous attempt.
- **Ambiguity.** A dispatch that timed out or was cut after DATA, or whose worker died mid-send, ends `AMBIGUOUS` and is never resent automatically. A `po.manage` holder confirms it or sends it again (a new attempt), both audited with a note; nothing else changes a dispatch's status by hand.
- **Audit.** Every attempt (start and outcome, abandoned and late ones) is an audit event on the order by `system:worker`, with the revision, attempt number and error stage, never the address or the content. Warehouse operators (`po.view`, who hold `supplier.view`) see the dispatches' status and address without the decision.

## Receiving: goods receipts and attribution (step M6.1)

Code in `apps/receiving/` and `apps/handovers/` (direct handovers); plan §3.4, Q9, §4.2-4.9.

- **Capabilities.** Posting needs `receipt.post`, voiding `receipt.void` (group administrators and material managers); `receipt.view` is the read capability (404 outside it, 403 inside it without the right: a warehouse operator or site manager voiding). A receipt is a document of its order's branch (S5); one at a worksite also belongs to that project. Posting is authorized on what the receipt will belong to: the order at a warehouse; at a worksite the requests of that project the order serves, whose procurement branch is the order's (C5), so each membership covers them exactly when it covers the receipt (S6). A site manager therefore receives only at an assigned project's worksite, never at a warehouse, and the goods go to that project's allocations only (Q9). Each service authorizes in full inside its transaction, with session MFA for privileged grants; a service-level matrix calls each operation as every actor of the shared fixture.
- **Where goods arrive (C7, S9).** A warehouse of the order's branch or of a branch it manages, or the worksite of a project with allocations on the order; active locations of active branches only; a closed project's goods arrive at a warehouse. The location and the project are read again after the access lock, so a project or branch closing at the same moment waits for the receipt or is seen by it. A location other than the ship-to is recorded in the audit event.
- **Quantities.** Nothing is received beyond what remains on a line (I4), on a line closed short or on an order that is not issued (C10). Attributions are capped by each allocation's ceiling and its request line's A - R (and A - D on site), a manual split by the same caps, so R and D never pass A (I5, I13); what is left is surplus. Posting and voiding lock the order's allocation component (L2-L5) first, then the receipt (L6) and its direct handover (L7), numbers last (L9): threaded tests race two receipts for the last quantity, two voids, a receipt against a line closure and the E13 case.
- **Never counted twice (I8).** Each site attribution has exactly one `DIRECT_RECEIPT` handover line with the same request line and quantity (a unique key in the database); a direct handover is created and voided only with its receipt.
- **Immutable documents (I11).** A database trigger lets a posted receipt or handover change only by its void (status, void fields), once, and refuses any update or delete of their lines and attributions, whatever writes; CHECK constraints keep the void fields together. The C1-C3 composite keys hold a receipt line on its receipt's order, an attribution on the received order line and a handover line on its handover's project. Integration tests write the forbidden rows through the ORM.
- **Requests.** A request with received goods is no longer cancelled or returned to draft, even once its order has closed (I13).
- **Projection.** `expected_deliveries` shows a project's incoming goods to who holds `receipt.view` on that project: the order number, the supplier's display name and the expected date as issued, the material, the unit and the quantity still expected for that project; never a price, the supplier's contact details or another project's quantity (tested with the sentinels). A branch that does not buy for the project sees none of its orders. The admin shows receipts and handovers read-only.

## Handovers from a warehouse (step M6.2)

Code in `apps/handovers/services.py` and `selectors.py`; plan §2.3 (point 3), §3.4, §4.5-4.9.

- **Capabilities.** Posting needs `handover.post` (group administrators, material managers, warehouse operators), voiding `handover.void` (group administrators, material managers); `handover.view` is the read capability (404 outside it, 403 inside it without the right: a site manager posting, a warehouse operator or site manager voiding). A handover is a document of its branch (S5), the procurement branch of every request it serves (C8), so posting is authorized on those requests: each membership covers them exactly when it covers the handover (S6). Each service authorizes in full inside its transaction, with session MFA for privileged grants; a service-level matrix calls both operations as every actor of the shared fixture.
- **Where material comes from (C7, S9).** An active warehouse of the handover's branch or of a branch it manages, never a worksite; the project must be `ACTIVE` and its branch, the requests' branch and their companies active. The project, branch and location are read again after the access lock.
- **Quantities (I5).** Each line takes at most its request line's outstanding quantity A - D, on an open line only (approved request, live line, C10). The request's whole allocation component is locked (L2-L5) before anything is read, and direct handovers of site receipts lock the same rows, so D never passes A: threaded tests race two handovers for the last quantity, a handover against a site receipt both ways, two voids and a void against a new handover. Warehouse stock is not tracked in phase 1: what a warehouse received and has not handed over is a warning (`stock_warnings`, for `handover.post` holders of that warehouse), never a limit.
- **Commitments.** D rising above R raises the commitment max(D, R) + P + Q, so the reconciliation cuts the pending above what is still needed, as audited trims; the freed quantity shows as *unallocated on order* for purchasing to decide.
- **Receiver.** A user must be active, not offboarded and hold `project.view` on the handover's project; otherwise the handover names the person in free text. A user id that does not qualify gets the same refusal as one that does not exist.
- **Reversal (I8, I11).** A `DIRECT_RECEIPT` handover is never voided on its own; a `FROM_WAREHOUSE` handover is voided with a reason, once, and the trigger of M6.1 keeps every other column and its lines frozen. A void reopens the requests it had closed (automatic closes only) and reconciles, so a trimmed commitment never revives.

## Receiving and handover screens (step M6.3)

Code in `apps/receiving/views/`, `apps/handovers/views.py` and the two `forms.py`; plan §3.4, §8.2.

- **Methods and capabilities.** Every action is its own POST-only URL (`@mutating`) with its GET page elsewhere; the checks run in S7's order (capability somewhere → 403, object in scope → 404, capability on it → 403) and the services authorize again. `receipt.view` reads receipts and the expected deliveries, `receipt.post` finds the order and posts, `receipt.void` voids; `handover.view` reads (a site manager reads their project's), `handover.post` posts, `handover.void` voids a warehouse handover. Every URL and method has rows in the acceptance matrix, with a warehouse and a worksite receipt and a warehouse and a site handover as positions.

The services validate everything again under their locks (C7, C10, I4, I5, §4.5 caps).
- **Site managers (§3.4).** They find an order through the live allocations of the request lines they see on issued or closed orders (never a draft), receive only at their project's worksite, and see on the expected deliveries, the receipt form and their receipts the order number, the supplier's display name, the expected date, the material, the unit and what their project can take; never the order's outstanding quantity, a price, the supplier's contact details or another project's quantity (matrix rows with the sentinels, for the page, the form and the receipt). Since M8.3 that holds for refusals too. An over-receipt is refused without the order line's outstanding quantity in the message, and only once every line and split is valid, so neither the message nor the order of the refusals tells how much the line holds for other projects.

- **Browser.** No inline script or style: the only script added is the `data-once` handler in `app.js`. Responses of these screens are `no-store, private` like every authenticated page; no token travels in a URL.

## Attachments (step M6.4)

Code in `apps/attachments/`; plan §5 "Attachments", §4.7 (L8), §9, Q8, Q22.

- **Parents and permissions.** A photo or PDF belongs to exactly one material request, goods receipt or handover: one nullable foreign key per type and a CHECK "exactly one set", never a generic relation. `attachment.*` are not capabilities; every operation is checked against the parent (`parents.PARENTS`): *view* with its view capability (`request.view`, `receipt.view`, `handover.view`); *add* with `request.create` by the requester on a draft, `receipt.post` or `handover.post` on a posted document (never a cancelled or voided one); *remove* by the uploader while the parent is a draft, otherwise with a reason by who holds `request.source`, `receipt.void` or `handover.void` there (group administrators, material managers and engineers). A removal is a soft delete; everything is audited on the parent (`attachment.added`, `attachment.removed`, `attachment.purged`, `attachment.storage_alert`). The views follow S7 (capability somewhere → 403, parent out of scope → 404, no right on it → 403) and the services authorize in full in their transaction. A trigger (`attachments.0002`) keeps an attachment immutable except for its one removal, so it can never be relinked, and lets a row be deleted only once removed.
- **Upload before the body (F73).** `views.upload` is the project's only `csrf_exempt` (allowlisted in `scripts/check_access_rules.py`). Before reading any byte of the body it refuses an unauthenticated session (a cross-site request carries no `SameSite=Lax` cookie), a parent the user cannot see or add to, a missing or foreign `Origin`, a missing or chunked length (411), a declared length above 10 MiB plus 64 KiB (413) and anything but `multipart/form-data`. It then installs `uploads.ReservingUploadHandler` as the only handler and calls an inner `csrf_protect` view: the CSRF check parses the body through the handler, whose `handle_raw_input` runs **admission** first. A CSRF failure, a broken body or any later refusal releases the reservation.
- **Admission, processing, finalization (F31, F45).** Admission (`services.admit_upload`) locks the parent, then `StorageQuota` (L8), and checks used + reserved + declared ≤ the limit (507), fewer than 10 files on the parent counting removed ones (until their purge) and uploads in progress (409), and fewer than 2 uploads in progress with a live lease installation-wide (429 with `Retry-After`); it inserts a `PROCESSING` reservation with a 15-minute lease. The body streams to the reservation's staging file and is cut as soon as it exceeds the reservation. Finalization (`services.finalize_upload`) authorizes again, locks parent → quota → reservation and stores the file only while the reservation is `PROCESSING` with an unexpired lease; a stored file larger than its reservation takes the difference from the quota under the same lock or is refused. Any failure marks it `RELEASED`, returns its bytes and deletes what was staged or stored. The upload form carries an idempotency key: a double tap stores one file and gives the second slot back. Threaded tests race uploads near the quota, to one parent, and the expiry against a finalization.
- **Malware scan (M8.3, ADR-0015).** Every file is scanned before it goes further, the original of an image included (`scanner.py`): `clamd` over a unix socket, with a 60-second deadline. It fails closed: no verdict means 503 with `Retry-After` and nothing stored. A file with malware gets 422, its slot is given back, and the refusal goes to the security log (`attachment_malware`) and the audit trail on the document (`attachment.malware_refused`), by signature, never by file name. What cannot be scanned is refused as well: past the scan limits, or a document with a password. Signature scanning only knows known malware; files stay served as sandboxed attachments.
- **Enforced time limits (F54).** Caddy's `read_body` timeout (120 s) cuts a stalled body; images are decoded in a child process (`decoder.py`, run as `python -I`, importing only the standard library and Pillow) with `RLIMIT_AS` 512 MiB, `RLIMIT_CPU` 60 s, a file-size limit and no core dump, killed after 60 s of wall time; database steps are bounded by `lock_timeout`. System check `gmat.E020` refuses a lease that does not outlast these limits, so an expired lease always means the work stopped. It takes Caddy's body timeout as 120 s: a longer `CADDY_READ_BODY_TIMEOUT` needs a longer lease.
- **The decoder apart from `web` (M8.3, ADR-0015).** In production that child runs in the `decoder` container (`decoder_service.py`, `decoder_client.py`): uid 1001, no network, no volume but its socket and a scratch tmpfs, no secret in its environment. An exploit in the decoder cannot read what `web` holds or send anything anywhere. What comes back is untrusted: `web` bounds its size and stores it only if it is WebP. Two conversions running at once share the uid, so one could read the other's image (accepted in ADR-0015). The worker releases expired leases hourly under the same locks; a late finalization then finds `RELEASED` and fails.
- **Files.** The type comes from the magic bytes only: JPEG, PNG, WebP or PDF; an SVG, HTML or script named like an image is refused, and an image is decoded only as the format its bytes announce. Images above 40 MP are refused from their header, before decoding (decompression bombs), then turned upright, reduced to 2560 px and re-encoded to WebP, which drops EXIF, GPS and every other metadata. PDFs are stored as they arrived and never rendered on the server. Files get a random 64-hex name in `ATTACHMENT_ROOT/files/` on the private `media` volume; the shown name is cleaned (no path, quotes or control characters) and carries the stored type's extension.
- **Download.** Only through `views.download`, for who can view the parent (404 otherwise, also for a removed file): `Content-Disposition: attachment`, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `Content-Security-Policy: sandbox; default-src 'none'`, and `no-store` like every authenticated response. Caddy has no access to the volume. The admin lists the rows read-only and never the files or their storage names.
- **Quota (Q22).** 5 GiB by default (`ATTACHMENT_STORAGE_LIMIT_MB`; the setting is the authority, applied at the next upload). Removed files count until the purge. Crossing 80 % raises an alert once (logged and audited) until the purge brings usage back below it.
- **Purge and backups (F48).** The worker's `attachment_media` task runs only while it holds `gmat:media-backup` (`pg_try_advisory_xact_lock`): a backup holding the lock postpones it. It deletes the rows of attachments removed more than 90 days ago (`ATTACHMENT_RETENTION_DAYS`) and queues their files; a queued file is deleted, and its bytes returned to the quota, after `MEDIA_PURGE_GRACE` (24 h, longer than any backup copy). The backup's manifest lists the stored attachments of its snapshot (storage key, size, SHA-256), the backup copies exactly those under the lock and checks each copy, and `restore --verify` checks the media copy against the manifest.
- **Containers.** `web` stores and the worker purges through the `media` volume; the backup mounts it read only; Caddy and the database never see it. The volume starts owned by uid 1000 and closed to others (the image's `/srv/media`). Caddy's body limit is 11 MiB. `web` gets 1 GiB for Gunicorn, and the `decoder` 1.2 GiB for two children. `clamd` holds about 1 GiB of signatures in memory (ADR-0015).

`manage.py check --deploy` passes with zero warnings under production settings, the compose stack's included. While an HSTS switch is off, production silences Django's warning about that one switch (`security.W004`, `W005`, `W021`) and nothing else (exception X3).

## Images, database roles and backups (step M1.4)

Sources: `docker/app/`, `docker/backup/`, `docker/postgres/initdb/`. CI builds both images and holds them to the Trivy gate (`trivy-image`).

- **Application image** (`docker/app/Dockerfile`): `python:3.14-slim` pinned by digest, stages `assets` (Tailwind standalone CLI, sha256-verified) → `builder` (`uv sync --frozen --no-dev`, hashes from `uv.lock`) → `runtime`, plus a `dev` target. Runs as uid/gid 1000 on a read-only root filesystem; source is owned by root; pip is removed; `collectstatic` runs with `config.settings.build` (no database, no secret). `entrypoint.sh` is the only entry point (`web`, `worker`, `worker-healthcheck`, `migrate`, `check`, `migrate-plan`, `verify-internal`, `post-restore-reconcile`, `healthcheck`, `manage`).
- **Gunicorn** (`gunicorn.conf.py`): `gthread`, access log off (Caddy is the access log), no control socket, and `FORWARDED_ALLOW_IPS` must list the edge subnet: it refuses to start with a wildcard or an empty value. Its error log uses the same JSON formatter and redaction filter as Django.
- **Database roles** (`10-roles.sh`, run once at database initialisation, idempotent): `owner` (owns the schema; used only by the one-shot `migrate`), `runtime` (SELECT/INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE only; owns nothing, so it cannot alter or drop tables, triggers or the audit table) and `backup` (`pg_read_all_data`, read-only sessions). All use `scram-sha-256`. The script refuses placeholder or short passwords, repeated names or passwords, and a role password equal to the bootstrap superuser's. `psql` reads the passwords from its environment (`\getenv`), so they never appear in the process list (M8.3). The database writes no slow-statement log: Django binds parameters on the client, so a logged statement would carry its values (names, addresses, notes) (M8.3).
- **Backups** (`docker/backup/`): every 6 hours and on demand (`backup-now [--label]`). A psql session takes the advisory lock `gmat:media-backup` (key `hashtextextended('gmat:media-backup', 0)`; the worker's media purge uses the same key with `pg_try_advisory_xact_lock`), opens a `REPEATABLE READ READ ONLY` transaction and exports its snapshot. `pg_dump -Fc --snapshot` leaves out the data of `django_session`, `usersessions_usersession` and `django_cache`, and the manifest queries (row counts of every table, migration head, newest audit event) run in the same snapshot, so dump and manifest agree under concurrent writes. The media directory, when there is one, is copied under the lock and checksummed. Files are mode 0600 in a 0700 directory and kept 3 days locally. The offsite upload uses restic with a fixed host and tags and **never prunes**; `forget`/`prune` run only from the maintainer's machine (plan §9).
- **`restore --verify`** (weekly, and on demand) restores the newest dump into a private PostgreSQL started inside the backup container on a unix socket (no network, no write right on the real database), then compares every row count, the migration head and the newest audit event with the manifest. Tables whose data is excluded must come back empty.
- **Exceptions** in `.trivyignore.yaml` are Debian base-image packages with no fixed version yet; each has a statement, an owner and an expiry at most 90 days away, and needs the maintainer's explicit approval in the PR.

## The compose stack (step M1.5)

Files in `docker_compose/gmat_m8/` (see its README); `tests/live` proves the points below against a running stack and the `compose-smoke` workflow runs it.

- **Networks.** One internal network per database client (`db_web`, `db_worker`, `db_backup`, `db_migrate`), so `web` and `migrate` share no network with any other client. The worker and the backup both use `egress`, a network with a way out (SMTP, offsite storage); neither listens on a port. `freshclam` alone uses `updates`, for ClamAV's mirror. `clamd` and the `decoder` have no network at all; `web` reaches each through a unix socket on a tmpfs volume only the two of them mount, read-only on `web`'s side. `edge` is internal and shared only by Caddy and `web` (no route out for `web`); Caddy alone sits on `ingress`, the one network that publishes ports. The database is never published (the development overlay publishes it on 127.0.0.1 only).
- **Hardening.** `cap_drop: ALL`, `no-new-privileges`, read-only root filesystem, memory and process limits and log rotation on every service; `web`, `worker`, `migrate`, `backup` and the local Mailpit run as uid 1000, the `decoder` as uid 1001, `clamd` and `freshclam` as their image's `clamav` user (uid 100). Caddy runs as root inside its container with only `NET_BIND_SERVICE` (its data volumes are root-owned; nothing else is writable).
- **Caddy.** Admin API off; `read_header` 10 s and `read_body` (`CADDY_READ_BODY_TIMEOUT`, 120 s) so a client that stops sending frees its Gunicorn thread; body limit `MAX_UPLOAD_SIZE`; no trusted proxies, so a client-sent `X-Forwarded-For` never reaches Django; `Server` header removed; HTTP/3 only where the UDP port is published. The admin prefix (`ADMIN_URL_PATH`, the same value in Django) is limited to `ADMIN_ALLOWED_CIDRS`.
- **Maintenance gate.** `flags/maintenance.on` makes Caddy answer 503 with the maintenance page for every public request while `web` keeps running; the same page replaces a missing `web` (502/503/504).
- **Log redaction.** Caddy's default and access logs replace the token paths (`/accounts/password/reset/key/…`, `/account/email-change/…`, `/invite/…`) and every query string with `REDACTED`, and drop `Referer`, `HX-Current-URL` and the response's `Location`. A token link opened without its trailing slash gets Django's 301 to the token URL; before M8.3 its `Location` reached the access log. Only paths under those prefixes are secrets to Caddy; Django's own filter also redacts any long random segment.
- **Secrets.** `init.sh` generates every secret (mode 0600, never printed, redrawn if it resembles a placeholder). A secret is defined once in `.env` and reaches the services by interpolation; Django-only settings are in `app.env`. Only `*.example` templates are committed.
- **Worker.** `db_worker` and `egress` only, `./flags` mounted read-only, a heartbeat healthcheck (`worker-healthcheck`, also while paused) and 75 s to stop, so a send in flight can finish. Local and CI stacks add Mailpit (`docker-compose.local-mail.yml`); production never loads it.

## Deploys and recovery (step M8.4)

`docker_compose/gmat_m8/deploy.sh`, `recover.sh`, `restore-drill.sh` and `ops/`; `tests/unit/docker/test_deploy_scripts.py` pins them against fakes, `tests/rehearsal` against real containers ([deploy-rehearsal.md](deploy-rehearsal.md)).

- **A release is whole or it does not open.** `verify-internal` also asks the malware scanner and the image decoder for an answer (ADR-0015). The drill starts neither: it stores no upload.
- **Nothing unverified runs.** `deploy.sh` checks the signature of `release-manifest.json` and of both images against the workflow's identity for that tag, checks the manifest against the version, repository and registry it expects, pulls by digest, and stops if the compose files would run anything else. A failure here changes nothing and leaves the site open. There is no flag that skips a signature check.
- **A resumed deploy remembers.** `deploy.sh --resume` counts its steps from the start, but the record keeps that the failed release migrated (`MIGRATION_RAN`), whether that was flagged irreversible and the first dump, so a fix-forward that fails early never reads as "nothing changed", and `recover.sh rollback` stays refused.
- **Two gates, one lock.** `flags/maintenance.on` (Caddy answers 503, whatever `web` is doing) and `flags/worker.paused` (the worker only writes its heartbeat) go on before the writers stop and off only at the commit point, after `verify-internal` passed against `web:8000` directly. Any failure stops with both on: no public write, no email. If the site or the worker does not answer once opened, both go on again. Deploys, recoveries and drills share one `flock`; a recovery takes it before it reads how the last deploy ended.
- **The token.** `GITHUB_READ_TOKEN` in `.env` reaches `curl` through stdin and `docker login` through `--password-stdin`, never an argument or a container. It is read-only and scoped to this repository (plan H8.6).
- **Before any restore** the failed database is kept: `preserved/<time>/failed.dump`, `failed-state.json` (outbox outcomes, access and credential events), `written-since.json` (for re-entry), mode 0600, and a renamed copy of the `pg_data` volume. These hold personal data and live until a human removes them.
- **After any restore** `post-restore-reconcile` runs before either gate opens (`apps/recovery`, plan §9):
  - a new `SECRET_KEY`, no fallback (`recover.sh`); every session and every row of the session list gone, and every `session_epoch` bumped; the MFA and outbox keys stay, so enrollments survive;
  - every pending invitation and email change revoked, every unsent security email wiped and expired;
  - every set of recovery codes deleted, because allauth derives them from a stored seed and a restore revives consumed or replaced ones (F52). Privileged accounts must generate a new set before anything else; others are told at sign-in;
  - the outbox quarantined (F44): anything unsettled, unless the failed database recorded a terminal outcome for that row. A human releases or discards what is left (`manage outbox_quarantine`: notifications in bulk, dispatches one by one, Q25); nothing is sent automatically;
  - with a failed state, the access-reducing and credential events the restored audit log lacks are replayed in order (`apps.recovery.replay`): revocations, shortened validity, offboardings, deactivations, projects stopped, MFA resets, removed factors, and password changes (an unusable password: the old hash may be the stolen one). Anything that gave access is reported and entered again by a human, so a restore never gives more than the snapshot had;
  - an email change among those events is held, never applied: the failed database may have been written with the runtime credentials, so a row alone must not hand an account to an address. The account stays suspended under its restored address until a human accepts or rejects the change (`manage confirm_account --email-change`); the operator running the reconciliation stays signed in, with the change in the report and not applied;
  - without one (the VPS is gone), every privileged account but the operator's is suspended until confirmed one by one (`manage confirm_account`, Q23), and the accounts the maintainer lists are offboarded or lose their credentials.

  A second restore to the same or an older dump gives nothing back either: the export carries the `recovery` events, and what the first restore replayed (`recovery.event_replayed`, with the original's data), suspended or was told about a gap (`recovery.gap_offboarded`, `recovery.gap_credentials_reset`, per account) is applied again. A confirmation is not: the account waits to be confirmed again.

  Password changes and removed factors reach the audit trail for this replay (`user.password_changed`, `user.authenticator_removed`), since a container's security log does not outlive it. Django's own `changepassword` command, run by hand on a shell, is the one path that writes no such event; the runbook says not to use it.
- **Offsite** (`docker_compose/offsite/`): the VPS's credentials cannot delete or overwrite anything, either a REST server in `--append-only` mode or an S3 key without delete rights. Pruning runs only from the maintainer's machine (`scripts/offsite_prune.py`). Forged snapshots appended from a compromised VPS can fill the weekly and monthly rules, so an older real snapshot loses its place (rehearsed); `--keep-within 90d` protects only what is inside the window. The script refuses a repository with a future timestamp, an unknown host or tag, an abnormal count per day, or a snapshot that is new since the last prune but dated before it (a past date is the forgery that looks like any other snapshot, so the evidence is the maintainer's own record of the last prune; a forgery dated after it still passes, during a compromise that makes those days suspect anyway), checks restic's plan against a window measured from its own clock, forgets exactly the snapshots it checked, and lists the repository again, since restic exits 0 when a server refuses a removal.
- **The drill** runs on its own project with every network internal and `OUTBOUND_EMAIL=off`, restores the same image digests from the offsite copy, and fails unless `web` and the worker cannot reach the outside (`restore-drill.sh`).

## The UI shell (step M1.6)

Templates in `templates/`, styles in `static/src/app.css`, code in `static/js/app.js` and `static/vendor/`.

- **No inline code.** Pages carry no inline script, style or event handler (`tests/unit/core/test_ui_shell.py` scans the rendered login, home and error pages; a real-browser run against the stack showed zero console and CSP errors). The only `<script>` blocks are allauth's `application/json` data blocks, which the CSP does not execute.
- **htmx** 2.0.11 is vendored (`static/vendor/README.md` records its source and checksum; a test pins the sha256). It is configured by `<meta name="htmx-config">`: `historyCacheSize: 0`, `refreshOnHistoryMiss`, no eval, no script tags, no indicator styles, `selfRequestsOnly`. The CSRF token travels in `hx-headers` on `<body>`; the cookie is `HttpOnly`. Nothing is written to `localStorage` (checked in the browser).
- **Static files** are hashed (`CompressedManifestStaticFilesStorage`) and served by WhiteNoise with a far-future cache header; the Tailwind input is not shipped.
- **Language**: Spanish by default, English and French; the choice is a `__Host-language` cookie (`Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax`) set by a POST form. The form's `next` is the current path, except on a token URL, where it is `/` so a secret is never copied into another form. Catalogs are in `locale/`; the `.mo` files are compiled in the image build.
- **Error pages** 400, 403 (including the CSRF failure), 404, 405, 409, 429 and 500 show a code and a generic sentence, never a path, a query, an exception or a user. 405, 409 and 429 are produced by `ErrorPagesMiddleware` for bare responses; 500 renders without a request.
- **Referrer policy.** `no-referrer` only on a URL that carries a token. A form page keeps `same-origin`: under `no-referrer` browsers send `Origin: null` on its POST and Django's CSRF check refuses it, so login would fail. (Found by logging in with a real browser; the M1.3 tests asserted the header, not the login.)

## Browser tests (step M8.2)

`tests/e2e` drives Chromium against the compose stack (the `e2e` workflow; `uv run pytest tests/e2e -m e2e` on a fresh stack, see CLAUDE.md). Besides the role flows, it proves in a real browser what the unit and live layers can only assert about headers:

- **CSP.** Every page a test opens is watched: a `securitypolicyviolation`, an uncaught script error or a 5xx answer fails the test that caused it (and one test proves the listener fires on an inline script).
- **History.** Sign out, another person signs in at the same browser, Back through the whole history: nothing of the first person's pages is shown (`no-store` responses, htmx's empty history cache, `refreshOnHistoryMiss`).
- **Sentinel, level 3 (plan §5).** A browser follows an invitation, a password reset and an email-change link, loads assets, makes htmx requests and goes back and forward, typing sentinel passwords on the way. No token or password appears in the Caddy, Gunicorn/Django (`web`) or worker logs, and no request carried a token in `Referer`, `HX-Current-URL` or `Origin`.
- **Phones.** Each role's daily screens are loaded at 360 px: none scrolls sideways (this found the audit list overflowing by 14 px, fixed in `app.css`).
