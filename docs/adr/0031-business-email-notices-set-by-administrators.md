# ADR-0031: Business email notifications: settings for everyone and per person, by group administrators

- **Status:** Accepted (the maintainer's requirement of 2026-11-16, issue #70, decisions Q87–Q91; implemented in PR #74; status recorded at the maintainer's review of PR #74, 2026-11-19)
- **Date:** 2026-11-18
- **Governs:** `apps/notifications/kinds.py`, `apps/notifications/models.py`, `apps/notifications/resolution.py`, `apps/notifications/eligibility.py`, `apps/notifications/recipients.py`, `apps/notifications/notices.py`, `apps/notifications/outbox.py`, `apps/notifications/selectors.py`, `apps/notifications/services/preferences.py`, `apps/notifications/views.py`, `apps/organizations/notice_forms.py`, `apps/organizations/notice_dialogs.py`, `apps/organizations/views/notification_screens.py`, `tests/e2e/test_notification_settings.py`

## Context

The application emails reviewers and requesters at every step of a request: a request to review, an assignment, an approval, a rejection, a reviewer's edits, goods handed over. The only control was each person's all-or-nothing *no business email* switch (M7.1). The pilot found that some people, and in some organisations everybody, do not want some of these emails: a group administrator receives every request to review of the group. Nobody but the person could change anything.

## Decision

1. **A registry of notices.** The business emails are six stable keys (`apps.notifications.kinds.Notice`): a request to review, a request assigned to me, my request approved, my request rejected, my request changed by a reviewer, goods handed over or received on site. Per notice, `NOTICES` says its label, who receives it, when, the capability it is received through and its order on the screens. Every subscriber of `notices.py` names its notice, and `queue_notification(notice=…)` records it on the message (`EmailOutbox.notice`). **Security mail** (invitations, email changes, password and second-factor notices, resets; ADR-0024) and **supplier dispatches** (ADR-0010) are not notices: they carry none, a CHECK keeps a notice to kind `NOTIFICATION`, and no setting ever filters them. A later business email joins the registry with a key of its own, on by default.
2. **Two levels of setting, stored per channel.** `NoticeDefault` is the setting for everyone (one row per notice and channel; no row means on, so nothing changes until an administrator decides). `NoticeSetting` is one person's setting (`ALWAYS` or `NEVER`; no row means follow the setting for everyone). The channel is `EMAIL` only; the planned Telegram bot adds its channel and its own consent to the same tables (Q90). Nothing of the bot is built.
3. **Who wins** (`apps.notifications.resolution`, one query): the person's own refusal of business email first, then the person's setting, then the setting for everyone. An administrator never overrides the person's own refusal: *always* has no effect on someone who turned business email off. The same rule is applied when a message is queued (nobody left means no message) and again at the claim (`eligibility.eligible_recipients`), so a message queued and not yet claimed follows a later change. A message already claimed may still leave: claims commit before I/O (ADR-0009). A message queued before the migration has no notice and follows the person's refusal alone. Who may receive the message at all (active, still holding its capability on its object) stays `eligibility`'s question.
4. **Who changes what.** `org.manage` (group administrators) with session MFA. `set_notice_default` changes the setting for everyone, installation-wide, as units of measure (ADR-0030). `set_person_notices` changes one person's settings, only for a person the administrator's people screens reach (out of scope is 404), never a staff or superuser account (the rule of `rename_user` and the MFA reset). An administrator may set their own, which answers the group administrator flooded by *A request to review* (ADR-0022 forbids changing one's own authority and address, not a preference). Both services follow the write order (authorize in the transaction, idempotency record, the users locked `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` in id order, rows `FOR UPDATE`), write only what differs, and record `notification.default_changed` or `notification.person_changed` with before and after, branchless, the actor by name. **Nobody is told** of a change. A change gives or takes away no access or credential, so `apps.recovery.replay.HANDLERS` has no handler for it (ADR-0016); the settings are business data, kept by backups.
5. **Screens.** *Administration › Email notifications* (`/org/notifications/`, `org.manage`): the six emails with who, when, the state for everyone and how many people have a setting of their own; one inline dialog each states what turning it off or on changes. The person's page has a section with each email's effective state and the level that decided it (the setting for everyone, set for this person, or the person turned business email off), and an inline dialog to change them; neither is offered on a staff account. `/account/notifications/` keeps the person's switch and shows, read-only, what applies to them and why. Every dialog follows ADR-0027 (a form posts on its own POST-only URL; GET and HEAD never write). What a person's settings show is read only by group administrators and by the person.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Per company, branch or role settings.** Not asked for; the person's setting covers the exception and the setting for everyone the rule. A level can be added later without reshaping the tables if the maintainer asks.
- **Let an administrator override a person's own refusal.** Business email is the person's to refuse; ADR-0024 keeps security mail outside every setting, and that is the only mail an administrator can insist on.
- **One setting for approvals and rejections.** A requester may want to keep only the rejections, so they are two notices.
- **Resolve only when queueing.** A message queued but not yet claimed would ignore a change made minutes later, and the administrator would see mail leave after turning it off.

## Consequences

- [ADR-0009](0009-no-redis-database-cache-outbox.md) (the outbox): `queue_notification` filters recipients and records the notice; the claim rule is unchanged. Checked, still holds.
- [ADR-0024](0024-session-idle-limits-own-sessions-credential-notices.md): security mail never carries a notice. Checked, still holds.
- [ADR-0029](0029-people-by-name-addresses-to-managers.md): the screens show people by name; an address only on the people screens administrators already have. Checked, still holds.
- [ADR-0030](0030-administration-in-the-app.md): an administration screen with its matrix rows, and its admin inventory entry. Checked, still holds.
- The next 0.Y.0 release: an expand-only migration (two tables and a blank column with a database default), so the previous version runs on the new schema.
- Each new business email adds a `Notice` and its registry entry; `queue_notification` takes the notice as a required argument and refuses an unknown one, so a subscriber cannot queue a business email without one.
